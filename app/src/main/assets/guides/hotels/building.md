Many native bees will use a bee hotel, as long as it gives them dry, safe nest tunnels that suit them. Recycled or scrap materials are a good choice.

## Holes

- A mixture of holes between 3 and 10 mm wide works best.
- Drilled holes should be 100 to 150 mm deep. If a hole is too short, the young bees that come out of it may be mostly of one sex.
- The cavities in bamboo should be 3 to 13 mm wide and about 15 cm long. Native bees are unlikely to use bamboo wider than 10 mm, though wasps and other insects may.

## Materials

- Hardwood suits drilled blocks, such as ironbark, jarrah and red gum.
- Cut bamboo so that each piece has one open end and one end closed by a node.
- Lantana canes about 20 cm long make good nests for reed bees.
- Blue-banded bees like to nest in soft clay.
- Please avoid timber that has been chemically treated, and choose materials that are not toxic.

## Where to put it

- Please put the hotel on a post, which keeps it out of the damp and makes it easier to watch.
- A place with good morning sun and shelter from strong winds suits it.
- A hotel with a weatherproof frame or roof can go out in the garden. Without one, please put it somewhere sheltered, such as on a verandah or under the eaves.

> Insecticides kill native bees as well as pests. If you have to use one, please keep it off the flowers, and use it very early in the morning or late in the afternoon, when bees are not foraging.
