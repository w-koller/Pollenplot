## Bees

- **Leafcutter bees**, about 6 to 15 mm long, nest in drilled holes and bamboo canes, and seal the nest with a series of leaf discs.
- **Resin bees**, about 6 to 16 mm long, nest in drilled holes and bamboo canes, and use resin to build cells inside the nest and seal its entrance.
- **Masked bees**, most of them less than 10 mm long, like the smaller holes, and close the nest with a shiny, flat, see-through wall like cellophane across the end of the tube.
- **Wasp mimic bees**, about 11 to 14 mm long, nest in drilled holes and bamboo canes, and build their cells and seal the entrance with a cellophane-like material.
- **Reed bees**, about 4 to 8 mm long, cut their own nest burrows in pithy stems. One bee often guards the tiny entrance by blocking it with her abdomen.
- **Blue-banded bees**, about 8 to 13 mm long, dig shallow nest burrows in clay bricks, and build clay cells for their young at the bottom.

## Wasps

Native solitary wasps very often share a bee hotel. Wasps catch insects to feed their young, such as caterpillars, aphids, cockroaches and spiders, where bees collect pollen. They can sting, but they are not generally aggressive.

- **Clay plugs.** A hole sealed with a creamy clay plug, or with a delicate clay funnel sticking out of it, belongs to a potter wasp, which catches caterpillars for its young.
- **Grass plugs.** A dense plug of long grass stems or grass seed heads belongs to a grass-carrying wasp, which catches crickets or grasshoppers for its young.
