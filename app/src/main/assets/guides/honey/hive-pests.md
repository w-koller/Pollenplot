Both pests do the most harm to weak colonies and to comb that no bees are guarding. A strong colony with a young, healthy queen and plenty of bees for its comb is the best defence against both.

## Small hive beetle

- Adults are 5 to 7 mm long, two to three times smaller than a honey bee, and yellowish or reddish brown to dark brown or black.
- Larvae are white with a brown head, about 10 mm long, with three pairs of short legs near the head.
- In an infested hive the honey ferments, so the comb looks wet and greasy and may smell like rotting oranges. A heavy infestation can make the colony die or leave.

Small hive beetle is found throughout New South Wales, Queensland, Victoria and the Australian Capital Territory, but not in the Northern Territory or Tasmania. If you find it somewhere it is not known, please contact your state or territory's department of agriculture straight away.

- Please keep bottom boards free of debris, where beetles hide and breed.
- Traps, bought or home-made, reduce the number of adult beetles.
- Please don't take comb with any brood into the room where you extract honey, and clear away dead bees, wax and cappings promptly.
- Supers stored at 10 °C or colder are safe from egg laying, and comb frozen at −13 °C or colder for six hours has no beetles left alive at any stage.

## Wax moth

There are two species, the greater wax moth (*Galleria mellonella*) and the lesser wax moth (*Achroia grisella*). The adults are small and grey; the greater is 13 to 19 mm long and the lesser 10 to 13 mm.

- The larvae burrow through comb and line their tunnels with silk, damaging brood comb and honey comb.
- Brood they partly uncap as they burrow is left bare, which is called bald brood.
- They chew cavities into frames, and their cocoons can fuse a box of stored combs together.

Wax moth mostly takes advantage of diseased or declining colonies and of combs in storage, especially somewhere dark, warm and poorly ventilated.

- Please keep the apiary clear of weak or dead colonies and of old combs nobody is looking after.
- Freezing comb below −7 °C for four to five hours kills every stage. Comb is at risk again once it is out of the cold, so please keep it dry and out of reach of moths afterwards.
- Chemical treatments are best avoided, because they risk contaminating the honey and the colony.
