Business Queensland notes that swarms come in spring to early summer, and that a swarm is usually placid and unlikely to sting if it is left alone.

## Keeping a colony from swarming

- The Code asks beekeepers to manage the space in a hive to manage swarming, because swarms can spread disease.
- Extension AUS's spring advice is to split hives, replace older queens and ease crowding.

## When a colony swarms

- Business Queensland asks beekeepers to take responsibility for swarms from their hives, and to catch a swarm as soon as it forms a cluster.
- Catching swarms into spare hive boxes lowers the risk of colonies settling in nearby buildings and trees, where they can become a nuisance.
- A local beekeeping club can advise on handling swarms.

## Swarm catch boxes

- A box set out to catch swarms may hold only foundation, not drawn comb, brood, honey or pollen.
- The Code counts a swarm catch box as a hive, so it needs your hive identification code.
- A swarm catch box away from where you live must also carry your name and a phone number, in letters at least 25 mm high.
