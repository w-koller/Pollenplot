Business Queensland's advice is to manage a backyard hive so that its bees do not become a nuisance to the neighbours. Please also check with your council, because councils can have their own rules for keeping bees.

## Your neighbours

- Please tell your neighbours before you set up a hive, and explain how you will look after it.
- Please face the entrance so that the bees fly across your own yard rather than straight into a neighbour's.
- Where that is not possible, a hedge, shrubs, or shade cloth on a trellis makes the bees fly up and over.
- Bees are drawn to lights, especially fluorescent ones, so a barrier between the entrance and a neighbour's lights helps.
- A local beekeeping club can help with keeping bees in town.

## Water and sun

- Please set out water before the bees arrive. Bees need it to live and to keep the hive at the right temperature, and the Code asks every beekeeper to give their bees water they can reach.
- Business Queensland suggests a sunny spot with water close by, such as wet sand or gravel, the edge of a concrete pond, or floating water plants. Bees with their own water are less likely to visit the neighbours' yards and pools.

## Fire

- Please keep the grass mown and clear branches, twigs and dead grass from around the hive, leaving a firebreak 2 metres wide.
- Please check the fire rules before you use a smoker where you live, and keep the smoker in a fireproof container in the fire season.

## What you bring in

The Code asks beekeepers to get bees, feed and equipment from a reliable source, to keep pests and diseases away from their bees.

- Please sterilise second-hand equipment before you use it.
- Please keep new bees apart from your other bees, and have them tested before you buy them or when they arrive, so that you know they are free of pests and diseases.
- Each frame needs to lift out on its own so that the hive can be inspected, and the hive must carry your hive identification code. The page on what every beekeeper must do has the rest of the Code's rules for hives.
