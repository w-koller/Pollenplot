The Australian Honey Bee Industry Biosecurity Code of Practice sets out what every beekeeper must do to keep pests and diseases from spreading between hives. It says what to achieve rather than how to keep bees, and your state or territory's laws apply alongside it.

## Register

Registration is compulsory in every state and territory. Please register with your state or territory, pay any fee it sets, and give an accurate count of your hives when you register. Your state's registration page is linked from the honey bee section of the Bees tab.

## Report

Please report any notifiable pest or disease you find or suspect to your state or territory by the quickest means you can, and follow a report made by phone with one in writing, such as an email or a text message. Each state and territory has its own list of notifiable pests and diseases, and the Code asks every beekeeper to know the list where their hives are.

## Inspect

Please look through each hive for its strength and for pests and diseases, in a way that is likely to find any that are there. Each inspection should take in at least the equivalent of three full-depth frames of brood, with the adult bees shaken off. The Code asks for this as often as needed to keep pests and diseases from spreading, and in any case at least three times a year, at least three months apart. Mite checks have their own page in this guide.

## Keep records

The Code asks every beekeeper to keep records of:

- the date of each inspection, how strong the hives were and any pests or diseases found;
- each mite check, with its method, date and result;
- treatments and anything else done about pests or diseases;
- honey tests for American foulbrood;
- hive movements, with the dates, the number of hives and where they went;
- bees, used hives and used equipment brought in, with the date and where they came from;
- biosecurity training.

Records can be on paper or electronic, and they must be kept for at least three years. Pollenplot keeps each hive's inspections, mite checks, treatments and colony events on its page.

## Look after hives and equipment

- Each hive needs intact outside surfaces, so that bees can only come and go through its entrances.
- Each frame must lift out on its own, so that the hive can be inspected. Top-bar hives are allowed when each comb can be lifted out separately.
- Each hive must carry your hive identification code, as your state or territory's laws require.
- A box set out to catch swarms may hold only foundation, not drawn comb, brood, honey or pollen.

## Keep robber bees away

- Please never leave a used hive, frames, comb, honey or wax where bees can rob it, including while you move it.
- Please don't abandon or neglect a hive, and please destroy or properly dispose of unwanted bees and hive parts.
- A weak hive must be managed so that it does not attract robber bees, and a dead hive must be removed straight away or made proof against them.
- Your bees need water they can reach.
- The Code also asks beekeepers to manage the space in a hive to manage swarming, because swarms can spread disease.
