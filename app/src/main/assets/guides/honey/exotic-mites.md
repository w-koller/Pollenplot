> Plant Health Australia asks anyone who sees mites on adult bees or in the brood to call the Exotic Plant Pest Hotline straight away, on 1800 084 881.

Two kinds of mite that harm honey bees overseas are not in Australia: Tropilaelaps mites and *Varroa jacobsoni*, a relative of *Varroa destructor*.

## Tropilaelaps mites

Tropilaelaps mites come from Asia, where they live on the brood of the giant honey bees. Two species, *Tropilaelaps clareae* and *T. mercedesae*, can also breed on the brood of European honey bees. They are not in Australia, and if they became established they would cause great losses to managed and wild colonies.

- They are active, red-brown mites, about 1 mm long and 0.5 to 1 mm wide, longer than they are wide, and big enough to see without a magnifying glass.
- Only 3 to 4% of them ride on adult bees, so they are hard to notice until there are many.
- Signs include an irregular brood pattern, deformed bees with missing legs or wings, and crawling bees and dead brood thrown out at the entrance.
- They spread when infested hives are moved and when bees drift between hives.

Two harmless creatures can look like them. The braula fly, found only in Tasmania, is red-brown, 1.5 mm long, covered in spiny hairs and has six long legs. Pollen mites are light brown and about 0.75 mm across.
