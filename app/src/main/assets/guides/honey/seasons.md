Extension AUS's advice is to manage a hive by what the local weather, the flowering and the bees are doing, rather than by the calendar, because conditions vary even across a valley. The seasons here are the Southern Hemisphere's.

## Spring

Extension AUS's spring advice covers these jobs.

- Check that the queen is laying and that the brood pattern is good, and look for brood diseases. Chalkbrood is a particular problem at this time of year.
- Look at the stores left from winter, and decide whether the colony needs feeding. Honey made from feeding should not be extracted.
- Decide whether to add a super or to take off winter honey.
- Manage swarming by splitting hives, replacing older queens and easing crowding. Business Queensland notes that swarms come in spring to early summer, and the swarms page in this guide has more.
- Combine weak colonies. Extension AUS's way is to kill one queen first and put newspaper between the two boxes.
- Change small hive beetle traps when they need it, and make sure there is water close by.

## The honey flow

- Please give the bees room for the nectar coming in. In a strong flow a super can fill in about a week, so please don't let a colony become honey-bound.
- Drawn comb saves the bees energy, compared with foundation.
- A single strong colony makes more honey than two weak ones, so please combine colonies when they need it.

## The cooler months

- Business Queensland advises against working bees in the cool times of the year.
- Agriculture Victoria's advice is to check the honey stores often through winter, and to boost the stores of a colony that is short for winter with syrup before the cold weather of autumn sets in.
- Extension AUS describes feeding sugar syrup or pollen in the cooler months to help colonies through winter. The feeding page in this guide has more.

## Checks through the year

- The Code asks for each hive to be inspected at least three times a year, at least three months apart.
- Where Varroa mite is not established, the Code asks for mite checks in spring, summer and autumn. Where it is established and the hive has brood, it asks for them every month. The Varroa page has the details.
