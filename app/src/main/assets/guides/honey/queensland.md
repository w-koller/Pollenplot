## What the law asks

- If you own one or more hives in Queensland, you must [register as a biosecurity entity](https://www.business.qld.gov.au/industries/farms-fishing-forestry/agriculture/biosecurity/entity-registration). Registration lasts three years and is free for hobby beekeepers. Native bee hives do not need to be registered.
- You must mark your hives with your hive identification number (HIN), at least one hive in every 50. Business Queensland recommends marking every hive, as proof that it is yours.
- Whenever you move bees or hives from where they are normally kept, you must make a movement record before you move them, and keep it for two years.
- You have a general biosecurity obligation to make sure your beekeeping does not spread a pest, disease or contaminant, and you are responsible for the health of your bees at every apiary site. You must take all reasonable and practical steps to meet it, and not meeting it is an offence.

> Please call Biosecurity Queensland on 13 25 23 straight away if you notice changes in your hives' health, such as scattered brood, chewed brood cells, crippled bees or fewer bees; if you have unexpected losses, such as a fall in production or a colony that suddenly collapses; or if you suspect a notifiable pest or disease, such as Varroa mite or American foulbrood.

## Hives in a backyard

- Please make sure the bees have water before you bring them home, and put the hives somewhere sunny.
- Please face the entrances so that the bees fly across your own yard rather than straight into a neighbour's.
- Please tell your neighbours before you bring bees home, and manage the hives so that the bees don't become a nuisance to them.
- Business Queensland suggests barriers such as hedges, shrubs, or shade cloth fixed to a trellis.
- Bees are drawn to lights, especially fluorescent ones.
- A local beekeeping club can help you learn more and manage bees in town.

## Working the hives

Please avoid working the bees in the cool times of the year, in rainy weather, and when there is little nectar and pollen about. Bees are under stress then, and can turn aggressive and start taking stored or spilt honey. Smoke calms bees, but please check the fire rules before you use a smoker in a residential area, and keep the smoker in a fireproof container during the fire season.

## Swarms

A swarm is a round or oval mass of bees looking for a place to start a new nest, led by a queen. Swarms come in spring and early summer, and the bees are usually placid and unlikely to sting when they are left alone. Please take responsibility for a swarm that has come from one of your hives and, whenever you can, catch it as soon as it has formed a cluster. A local beekeeping club can advise you on handling swarms.
