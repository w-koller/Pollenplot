A hive opened at a bad time makes for defensive bees, and honey left about draws robbers. Business Queensland's advice and the Code cover when and how to work a hive.

## When to open a hive

- Business Queensland advises against working bees in the cool times of the year, in rainy weather, or when little nectar and pollen is about. Bees can become savage then, and rob stored or spilt honey.
- Please ask your neighbours to stay inside while you work the hive, or agree with them on a time that suits.

## Smoke

- Smoke calms bees. Please check the fire rules before you use a smoker where you live.
- Please smoke the entrance before you use a noisy machine near the hive, such as a mower or a whipper snipper, or when a neighbour is about to.

## What to look for

- Extension AUS's spring advice is to check that the queen is laying and that the brood pattern is good, and to look for brood diseases.
- The Code asks for each inspection to take in at least the equivalent of three full-depth frames of brood, with the adult bees shaken off, so that any pest or disease is likely to show.
- The brood diseases page in this guide says what to look for, and the Varroa page how to check for mites.
- Pollenplot keeps each inspection on the hive's page: the queen and eggs, the brood pattern, the bees' temper, frames of bees and stores, queen cells and any signs of disease.

## Robber bees

- The Code says beekeepers must not let bees reach spilt honey, or leave used frames, comb, honey or wax where bees can rob them.
- A weak hive must be managed so that it does not draw robber bees.
