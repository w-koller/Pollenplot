The Food Standards Code describes honey as the sweet substance bees make from the nectar of blossoms or from plant secretions, which they store and leave in the comb to ripen and mature.

## Honey for sale

- Food sold as honey must be honey, with no less than 60% reducing sugars and no more than 21% moisture.
- Frames of honey made from feeding bees do not meet the legal standard, so please don't extract them.

## While you take it

- Please never leave frames, comb, honey or wax where bees can rob them, including while you carry them away, as the Code asks.
- To keep small hive beetle out, BeeAware advises against taking comb with any brood into the room where you extract honey, and asks for dead bees, wax and cappings to be cleared away promptly.
- Business Queensland advises against taking old combs, cappings or slum gum to a waste facility unless they will be buried straight away. Burning is the best way to destroy them when you are not rendering the wax.

## Honey tests for 20 or more hives

Beekeepers who manage 20 or more hives must have their hives tested for American foulbrood at least once in every 12 months. If you don't send honey to a honey packer, the Code asks for pooled honey samples from at least a fifth of your hives, chosen at random, to go to an approved laboratory. The samples can be taken while you extract honey.
