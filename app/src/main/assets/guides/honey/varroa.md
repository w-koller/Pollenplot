Varroa mites are tiny red-brown parasites of honey bees. An adult female is oval and flat, about 1.1 mm long and 1.5 mm wide. Most of the harm comes from the viruses the mites carry, such as deformed wing virus. A heavy infestation can build up within three to four years, with scattered brood and crippled, crawling bees, until the colony collapses.

*Varroa destructor* was found in New South Wales in June 2022. Where it is established changes, so please check your state or territory's page. *Varroa jacobsoni*, its relative, is not in Australia.

## How often

- Where Varroa is established, the Code asks for a mite check every month, all year, while the hive has brood.
- Where it is not established, the Code asks for at least one hive in each apiary to be checked in spring, summer and autumn.
- Queensland asks beekeepers to check their hives every month, and before, during and after a treatment.

## How to check

The Code accepts an alcohol wash, a soapy water wash, a sugar shake or drone uncapping. Drone uncapping shows whether there are mites, but it cannot tell you whether a treatment is needed. Business Queensland recommends the alcohol wash, done like this:

1. Pour half a cup, about 125 mL, of methylated spirits or ethanol stronger than 70% into a shake jar.
2. Choose one or two of the middle three frames of the brood nest.
3. Collect half a cup of bees, about 300, and put them in the jar. Please make sure the queen is not among them.
4. Close the jar with its solid lid and shake it hard for at least 15 seconds.
5. Pour the alcohol through a filter paper or cloth, then wash the bees twice more.
6. Look for mites on the filter, with a magnifying glass if you need one.

The bees in the jar die. A sugar shake is less reliable at finding mites, and some of its bees die later.

## After the check

- Please record each check with its method, date and result. Pollenplot works out the mites per 100 bees from your count.
- In Queensland, please report every hive check on the [Bee 123 form](https://www.business.qld.gov.au/industries/farms-fishing-forestry/agriculture/animal/industries/bees/varroa-mite/report-hive-check), even when you find no mites.
- If you find a mite you think is Varroa, please take a photo of it and keep the filter paper or cloth in a sealed plastic bag.
- Please use only authorised varroacides, always as the label says, and follow its safety and withholding instructions.

> Please report a mite you think is Varroa to your state or territory, or call the Exotic Plant Pest Hotline on 1800 084 881.
