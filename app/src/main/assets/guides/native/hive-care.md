An established hive needs very little looking after, but it does need a good place. Stingless bees cannot cool their nest as honey bees do, by spreading water through it and fanning their wings, so where the hive sits decides how hot it gets.

## Where to put it

- Please keep the hive in shade, under a shrub or tree, on a back deck or beside the house.
- It can have morning sun until about 10 am, but it needs shade for the rest of the day.
- In summer, please keep it out of direct sun from mid-morning to late afternoon.
- In winter, you can move it a metre into some morning sun, which warms the bees up and gets them going for the day.

## Hot days

Even a hive in a good position can heat to dangerous levels on days above 38 °C. In laboratory experiments, *Tetragonula carbonaria* died after 10 minutes at 39 °C inside the hive, and in research in Sydney in 2013 and 2014 many colonies died when it reached 45 °C outside. On any day over 39 °C, Cumberland City Council recommends one of these:

- Drape a wet towel over the hive, with one or both ends in a bucket of water. Please take the hive out of any outer box first, and drape the towel over the hive itself.
- As a last resort, on the most extreme days, close the entrance the evening before and keep the hive in an air-conditioned room for the day. You may lose some bees, but you will save the colony.

> Lines of workers on the front of the hive, fanning their wings and spreading out from the entrance, are a sign that it is overheating. Please cool it straight away.

## Cold days

A hive is quieter in winter, and *Tetragonula carbonaria* stays inside when it is below 18 °C. To check that a quiet hive is alive:

- look for pellets of waste on the ground just in front of it, which the bees drop when it is too cold to go out;
- look for a few guard bees at the entrance;
- put your ear to the entrance and listen for buzzing.

## Moving a hive

Foraging bees fly back to where their hive was, so a hive is moved either no more than a metre at a time or at least 1 km away, beyond their foraging range. The council prefers moving it a metre at a time, over several days if it has further to go. To use the long way, close the entrance the night before, leave the hive at least 1 km away for a week, then bring it back to its new place.
