## Splitting

An OATH box comes in two halves, which lets the colony be split. The two halves of a full hive are separated, and each is given a new empty half. Only a box made in two halves can be split.

Splitting raises the risk of pests getting in, and done wrongly it can lose both hives. One half may also fail to raise a new queen, so please watch both hives afterwards for signs of one, such as a mating swarm or the hive getting heavier.

## Budding

Budding works with any box. An empty box is joined to the entrance of a full hive, and over time the bees build a new nest in it. It usually takes about six months and can take up to a year, with a look about every two weeks. It is slower than splitting but less disturbing, with less risk of pests from broken pollen and honey pots, and little risk of losing the first hive.

## When a hive is ready

Either way, the colony needs to be strong and healthy, because the bees will not build a new nest otherwise. Cumberland City Council splits a hive only 18 months after the keeper received it, once it weighs at least 7 kg and has about 30 to 60 bees coming home a minute on a warm day.
