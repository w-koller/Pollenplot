Colonies of Australia's stingless bees, *Tetragonula* and *Austroplebeia*, can be kept in hive boxes. *Tetragonula carbonaria* is a small black bee that lives in a social colony with a queen, workers and males.

## A colony

- The queen is the only fertile female. Once she has mated, she spends the rest of her life in the hive laying eggs, for a year or longer.
- A hive can hold up to 10,000 workers, sterile females who do the colony's work and live for about 100 days.
- Males are there to mate with a new queen.
- A bee takes about 50 days to grow from larva to adult.
- The colony gathers nectar for energy, pollen for protein and other nutrients, and resin and plant material to build its nest, so a variety of flowering plants nearby is essential.

## Weather

*Tetragonula carbonaria* does not leave the hive when it is below 18 °C outside, so in winter a hive can go a few days with no bees outside, even though you can still hear them buzzing inside. Above 40 °C the nest can start to melt, and above 45 °C the bees can die. The page on looking after a hive explains how to keep one cool.

## Boxes

The most common box for stingless bees in Australia is a small wooden box called an OATH (Original Australian Tetragonula Hive). It comes in two halves, so that the colony can be split, and a compartment can be added on top for honey. The timber is usually 25 mm thick, and timber up to 45 mm thick gives the colony more insulation. A one-part box with insulation built in can be budded but not split.

> In Queensland, native bee hives do not need to be registered.
