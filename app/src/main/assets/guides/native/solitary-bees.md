Australia has more than 1600 species of native bee. They are solitary, semi-social or social, and most are solitary or semi-social; only a handful of social species make honey. A solitary female nests alone. She digs a burrow in soil or wood, or builds cells of resin or mud, stocks each cell with pollen and honey, and lays an egg in it. Most solitary bees can sting.

About 30% of Australia's solitary and semi-social bees nest in pithy stems or in holes already in timber, and many of them will use a bee hotel.

## Some well-known native bees

- **Blue-banded bees**, about 8 to 13 mm long, are solitary. They dig shallow nest burrows in soft clay, and each female builds clay cells for her young at the bottom of her burrow.
- **Leafcutter bees**, about 6 to 15 mm long, are solitary. They nest in drilled holes in timber and in bamboo canes, and seal the nest with a series of leaf discs.
- **Resin bees**, about 6 to 16 mm long, nest in drilled holes in timber or in bamboo canes. They use resin to build cells inside the nest and to seal its entrance.
- **Masked bees**, most of them less than 10 mm long, like the smaller holes. They close the nest with a shiny, flat, see-through wall like cellophane across the end of the tube.
- **Wasp mimic bees**, about 11 to 14 mm long, nest in drilled holes and bamboo canes, and use a cellophane-like material to build their cells and seal the entrance.
- **Reed bees**, about 4 to 8 mm long, are semi-social. They cut their own nest burrows in pithy stems, and one bee often guards the tiny entrance by blocking it with her abdomen.
- **Great carpenter bees** and **metallic carpenter bees** are semi-social too.

## Helping them nest

- A mixture of holes between 3 and 10 mm wide, drilled 100 to 150 mm deep in hardwood that has not been chemically treated, suits many of the bees that nest in holes.
- Pieces of bamboo with one open end and one closed by a node make good nests, and so do lantana canes about 20 cm long, for reed bees.
- Blue-banded bees like to nest in soft clay soil.
- Please avoid insecticides, which kill native bees as well as pests. If you have to use one, please keep it off the flowers, and use it very early in the morning or late in the afternoon, when bees are not foraging.
